# SHMIFTING KITCHEN
## Design Book

**Status:** Visual source of truth  
**Audience:** Claude Code, designers, illustrators, asset generators, and future contributors  
**Companion document:** `SHMIFTING_PRODUCT_BIBLE.md`

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines how Shmifting looks, feels, moves, and communicates visually.

The Product Bible defines **what the product does**.

This Design Book defines:

> **What world does this product belong to?**

Shmifting must never look like a conventional web application that has been decorated with festival illustrations afterward.

The goal is to create a coherent visual universe and build a functional product **inside that universe**.

---

# 2. The Golden References

The Golden References included with this Design Book are the highest-level visual source of truth.

## Golden Reference 01 — Original Style Reference

This is the original visual reference selected for Shmifting.

It defines the foundational visual language:

- dark charcoal canvas;
- dense illustrated composition;
- pastel psychedelic colors;
- strong black outlines;
- flat illustration;
- hand-drawn imperfection;
- surreal objects;
- eyes, hands, plants, celestial objects and strange creatures;
- controlled visual chaos;
- playful typography;
- unusual scale relationships;
- objects behaving in unexpected ways;
- poster-like composition;
- visual discovery.

### Important

Do **not** copy the literal subject matter from this image.

Copy its **visual grammar**.

The reference answers questions such as:

- How dense can a composition feel?
- How should objects relate to one another?
- How imperfect should shapes feel?
- How does pastel color behave against charcoal?
- How can surrealism feel playful rather than disturbing?
- How can many objects coexist without feeling like generic decoration?

# 3. Golden Reference Rule

Golden References are **not mood-board inspiration**.

They are visual constraints.

When implementing a new screen, component, illustration, or interaction:

1. Study the Golden References.
2. Identify the visual rules that apply.
3. Use those rules.
4. Do not introduce an unrelated visual language because it is easier to implement.

When written instructions and personal visual preference conflict:

> **Prefer consistency with the Golden References.**

Claude is implementing an established visual language.

Claude is not being asked to reinvent Shmifting.

# 5. The World

Shmifting exists in a strange little universe born somewhere between:

- a desert camp;
- a psychedelic poster;
- a communal kitchen;
- an illustrated dream;
- a handmade gift;
- and a group of friends who do not take themselves too seriously.

Everything may have personality.

A pepper may have an eye.

Steam may become stars.

A hand may grow from a cooking pot.

A moon may drink coffee.

A tomato may have legs.

A mountain may secretly be pasta.

A bottle may wander through the desert.

But this must never become random visual noise.

The surrealism should always feel like it belongs to **Shmifting**.

# 7. Visual Hierarchy of Themes

When multiple themes compete for prominence, use this order:

**1. People**

**2. Human connection / gifting**

**3. Camp participation**

**4. Desert**

**5. Kitchen / food**

This hierarchy does not mean food is visually unimportant.

It means:

> The kitchen exists for the people.

Never accidentally communicate:

> The people exist to operate the kitchen.

# 9. Color Behavior

Color should feel **distributed**, not branded around one corporate primary color.

Shmifting does not have:

> blue = primary action

Instead, color is part of the world.

Different objects may use different palette members.

However, functional states must remain consistent.

Examples:

- danger;
- allergy warning;
- success;
- incomplete state;

must remain understandable regardless of decorative color.

Do not sacrifice meaning for palette variety.

---

# 10. Avoid

Do not introduce:

- neon cyberpunk gradients;
- glossy gradients;
- glassmorphism;
- metallic UI;
- corporate blue;
- pure white SaaS backgrounds;
- luxury black-and-gold branding;
- generic festival rainbow gradients;
- excessive glow effects.

The world is psychedelic because of **illustration and composition**, not because every surface has a gradient.

---

# 11. Illustration Style

Illustration is foundational.

Preferred characteristics:

- flat;
- graphic;
- bold;
- outlined;
- slightly imperfect;
- hand-drawn;
- surreal;
- expressive;
- detailed without realism.

Objects should feel intentionally illustrated rather than mathematically constructed.

---

# 12. Line Work

Black or near-black outlines are a major part of the visual language.

Lines should:

- clearly define forms;
- occasionally feel slightly irregular;
- remain confident;
- vary subtly where useful;
- reinforce the handmade feeling.

Avoid ultra-perfect geometric vector illustration.

Avoid overly sketchy pencil illustration.

The target sits between:

**clean vector**

and

**human drawing**.

---

# 13. Shape Language

Prefer:

- blobs;
- imperfect circles;
- rounded organic geometry;
- distorted stars;
- curved hands;
- irregular labels;
- wavy frames;
- strange plants;
- imperfect containers.

Avoid excessive reliance on:

- perfect rectangles;
- perfect circles;
- rigid grids;
- generic rounded SaaS cards.

Geometry may be clean when functional clarity requires it, particularly inside Kitchen HQ.

---

# 14. Surrealism Rule

The world becomes distinctly Shmifting when ordinary objects behave unexpectedly.

Examples:

- eyes inside food;
- hands emerging from objects;
- celestial objects interacting with kitchen objects;
- food behaving like characters;
- impossible object combinations;
- tiny scenes inside larger objects;
- unexpected scale.

However:

> **Surrealism should create delight, not confusion.**

Do not make functional controls difficult to recognize merely to make them strange.

---

# 15. Weird, Not Childish

This is a critical rule.

Shmifting is playful.

It is not a children's product.

Avoid:

- preschool character proportions;
- overly cute facial expressions everywhere;
- baby-like mascots;
- toy-like interfaces;
- excessive bouncing;
- childish reward animations;
- cartoon voices associated with children's media.

Prefer:

- dry humor;
- strange expressions;
- unexpected juxtapositions;
- surreal adult poster art;
- visual jokes;
- playful imperfection.

---

# 16. Human Presence

The product should not become an abstract world made entirely of objects.

People matter.

Illustrated people may appear:

- cooking;
- sitting;
- giving;
- carrying;
- eating;
- talking;
- celebrating.

They do not need detailed realism.

Their role is to keep the visual world grounded in **human connection**.

# 18. The Memory Vial

- inside hands;

# 24. Composition

The Golden Reference uses **controlled density**.

This is essential.

Large atmospheric areas may contain many illustrated objects.

The eye should discover new details over time.

However, web interfaces need breathing room for interaction.

Therefore use two density modes.

---

# 25. World Density

Used for:

- landing areas;
- Home hero;
- menu reveal;
- voting hero;
- empty states;
- transitions;
- major milestones.

Characteristics:

**High illustration density**

**Strong composition**

**Poster-like**

**Layered**

**Surprising**

---

# 26. Interface Density

Used for:

- forms;
- tables;
- shopping;
- recipes;
- budget;
- allergy management;
- Kitchen HQ.

Characteristics:

**Lower illustration density**

**High readability**

**Strong structure**

**Selective Shmifting motifs**

The visual world remains present through:

- typography;
- borders;
- palette;
- labels;
- small illustrations;
- section artwork;
- micro-interactions.

---

# 27. The 80/20 Rule

For the Shmifter experience:

> **Approximately 80% world / 20% interface feeling.**

This does not literally define pixel coverage.

It describes perception.

A Shmifter should feel like they entered the Shmifting world and discovered functionality inside it.

---

# 28. The 25/75 Rule

For Kitchen HQ:

> **Approximately 25% world / 75% functional interface feeling.**

The Admin experience should clearly belong to Shmifting.

But the Kitchen Lead may spend significant time working there.

Clarity wins.

Never force operational information into decorative compositions that make work harder.

---

# 29. Fundamental Product Design Rule

# DO NOT BUILD A SAAS DASHBOARD AND DECORATE IT.

This is one of the most important instructions in the entire Design Book.

Incorrect approach:

1. Build conventional cards.
2. Add charcoal background.
3. Add psychedelic icons.
4. Add illustrations around the edges.
5. Call it Shmifting.

Correct approach:

1. Start from the Shmifting world.
2. Understand the task.
3. Design an interaction that naturally belongs inside that world.
4. Preserve usability.
5. Introduce conventional interface patterns only when they genuinely improve comprehension.

---

# 30. Poster Became Interactive

The ideal mental model for the member-facing experience is:

> **A psychedelic illustrated poster became interactive.**

Elements should sometimes feel embedded inside scenes.

A hand may present a button.

A sign may contain a status.

A flame may become a voting token.

A sun may indicate morning.

A pot may frame a menu.

This creates integration between world and product.

# 31. Typography Philosophy

Typography needs two personalities.

## Display Typography

Used for:

- SHMIFTING;
- major headings;
- campaign moments;
- voting titles;
- reveals;
- countdowns;
- major milestones.

It may be:

- expressive;
- irregular;
- bold;
- psychedelic;
- hand-drawn in feeling.

## Functional Typography

Used for:

- forms;
- recipes;
- ingredients;
- allergies;
- quantities;
- tables;
- shopping;
- budget;
- long text.

It must be:

- highly readable;
- clean;
- excellent in Hebrew;
- excellent in RTL;
- usable at small sizes.

Do not use expressive display typography for dense operational content.

---

# 32. Hebrew Typography

Hebrew is a first-class design requirement.

Never treat Hebrew as an afterthought.

Typography must support:

- natural RTL;
- correct punctuation behavior;
- numbers inside RTL interfaces;
- mixed Hebrew/English phrases;
- readable tables;
- readable forms;
- responsive text.

A beautiful Latin typeface that produces poor Hebrew is unacceptable.

---

# 33. English Usage

English is part of the camp voice.

Examples:

**SHMIFTING**

**GIFT OR SHMIFT?**

**THE GREAT MENU VOTE**

**THE MENU HAS SPOKEN**

**LOCK THE KITCHEN**

Use English for brand moments.

Use Hebrew naturally for actual communication with camp members.

Do not turn every button into English simply because English appears visually cool.

---

# 34. Text Hierarchy

Major screens should have strong editorial hierarchy.

Prefer:

**Large statement**

↓  

**Short human explanation**

↓  

**Primary interaction**

↓  

**Secondary details**

Avoid filling the top of screens with navigation chrome, breadcrumbs, controls, filters, and utility elements unless the task genuinely requires them.

---

# 35. Cards

Cards are allowed.

Generic cards are not.

A Shmifting card may use:

- irregular silhouette;
- illustrated frame;
- imperfect outline;
- label shape;
- pastel surface;
- small decorative object;
- integrated illustration.

However, cards containing dense operational information should become simpler.

The goal is not to make every rectangle strange.

---

# 36. Buttons

Primary buttons should feel physical and intentional.

Possible visual metaphors:

- sticker;
- label;
- sign;
- illustrated tab;
- hand-held object;
- stamp-like shape.

Buttons still need to behave like buttons.

Interaction clarity is mandatory.

Avoid generic framework-default buttons.

---

# 37. Forms

The food profile contains important information.

Forms should feel warm rather than bureaucratic.

Prefer:

> **איך אתם אוכלים?**

over:

> Dietary classification

Controls may use illustrated selection states.

However:

- labels must remain clear;
- errors must remain clear;
- keyboard interaction must work;
- selection must be obvious.

Allergy fields should become visually more serious when needed.

---

# 38. Voting

Voting is one of the strongest opportunities for immersive design.

The interaction should communicate:

> **Give your fire to the food you want.**

Meal options may feel like illustrated posters, plates, scenes, or objects.

Flame tokens should feel tangible.

Interaction feedback can include:

- flame movement;
- subtle reaction from the selected meal;
- small sparks;
- character reaction.

Do not turn voting into a slot machine.

---

# 39. Shifts

Shifts should communicate time visually.

Useful motifs:

☀ morning

🌤 daytime

🌙 evening

Capacity should remain obvious.

Example:

`3 / 4`

is more important than decorative cleverness.

Understaffed shifts should visually request help.

Full shifts should feel satisfying.

---

# 40. Final Menu Reveal

This is a celebration.

The final menu should not look like Admin output.

Treat it like:

> **The food lineup for Shmifting.**

Each major dinner concept may receive dedicated artwork.

Days can feel like festival poster sections.

Reveal moments may use stronger motion and richer illustration than everyday workflow screens.

---

# 41. Kitchen HQ

Kitchen HQ should feel like:

> **A serious tool built inside the Shmifting universe.**

Not:

> A psychedelic poster containing spreadsheets.

Prioritize:

- clear information hierarchy;
- fast scanning;
- useful filters;
- readable numbers;
- obvious warnings;
- strong tables where appropriate;
- efficient editing.

Decorate the **environment**, not every data point.

---

# 42. Tables

Tables are appropriate in Kitchen HQ.

Do not avoid them merely because they are conventional.

A good table is sometimes the correct design.

Style them through:

- typography;
- subtle palette;
- borders;
- labels;
- section framing;
- occasional illustration.

Never sacrifice comparison efficiency for novelty.

---

# 43. Allergy Visual Language

Allergy information must immediately stand apart from playful decoration.

It should communicate:

**ATTENTION**

not:

**FUN**

Avoid using humorous characters or jokes around safety-critical allergy warnings.

The surrounding product may remain Shmifting.

The warning itself must be unambiguous.

---

# 44. Budget Visual Language

Budget should remain calm.

Do not gamify money excessively.

Important numbers should dominate.

Useful concepts:

- total budget;
- projected;
- actual;
- remaining;
- cost per person.

Illustrative decoration may exist around the section, but financial comprehension comes first.

---

# 45. Shopping Visual Language

Shopping can regain more personality.

Ingredients are visually rich.

Possible motifs:

- strange shopping bag;
- vegetables escaping;
- ingredients with personality;
- illustrated category headers.

Individual shopping rows should remain highly efficient.

The Kitchen Lead may need to scan dozens of them.

---

# 46. Status Design

Statuses should communicate meaning through more than color.

Use combinations of:

- icon;
- label;
- color;
- position;
- wording.

Examples:

**DONE**

**NEEDS YOU**

**FULL**

**MISSING 2**

**REVIEW**

**ALLERGY CONFLICT**

Never depend exclusively on red/green differentiation.

---

# 47. Progress

Progress should feel meaningful.

Member progress may be playful.

Kitchen readiness should be operational.

Avoid meaningless animated progress rings purely for visual excitement.

Progress should answer:

> **What remains?**

---

# 48. Motion Philosophy

The world should feel alive even when nobody is touching it.

Motion should generally be:

- subtle;
- slow;
- ambient;
- organic;
- occasional.

Examples:

- steam drifting;
- eye blinking;
- plant moving slightly;
- star twinkling;
- sun breathing;
- flame flickering;
- dust moving;
- small creature reacting.

The goal:

> **Life, not distraction.**

---

# 49. Interaction Motion

User actions may create stronger responses.

Examples:

Voting:

A flame travels toward a meal.

Completing profile:

A small illustrated celebration.

Joining a shift:

Characters make room.

Locking the kitchen:

A larger final transition.

Motion should reinforce the meaning of the action.

---

# 50. Avoid Motion Abuse

Do not:

- bounce everything;
- animate every scroll event;
- create constant parallax everywhere;
- use motion that interferes with reading;
- use long animations before functional actions;
- force users to wait for decorative sequences.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

---

# 51. Sound

Sound is atmospheric, not mandatory.

The product may eventually support optional background audio or subtle sound effects.

Rules:

- no aggressive autoplay;
- user controls sound;
- silence is always valid;
- functional tasks must not require sound.

Possible atmosphere:

- desert ambient;
- subtle downtempo;
- warm psychedelic soundscape;
- gentle kitchen sounds.

Avoid turning the product into a music player.

---

# 52. Photography

Photography is secondary.

The main visual world is illustrated.

Photography may later appear for:

- actual camp members;
- memories;
- profile images;
- documentation.

Do not mix generic stock festival photography into the core illustrated experience.

---

# 53. Icons

### 1. Shmifting-specific illustrated symbols

### 2. Simple custom functional symbols

### 3. Conventional iconography where clarity requires it

Do not automatically use a generic icon library for every concept.

Especially avoid allowing generic icons to dominate member-facing screens.

# 55. Asset Consistency

When generating new assets:

Do not ask merely for:

> psychedelic tomato

The request must include the Shmifting visual context and Golden References.

New assets must match:

- line weight;
- palette;
- flatness;
- texture;
- weirdness level;
- character treatment;
- visual density.

A technically beautiful asset that belongs to another universe should not be used.

---

# 56. Claude and Asset Creation

Claude should not attempt to replace missing illustrated assets with:

- random emojis;
- generic stock imagery;
- unrelated icon packs;
- crude CSS drawings;
- a different illustration style.

If an important Shmifting asset is missing:

**mark the asset requirement clearly.**

Use an intentional temporary placeholder if necessary.

Do not silently invent a new visual language.

---

# 57. Responsive Design

Responsive behavior is not simply:

> shrink desktop.

Member-facing experiences should be designed mobile-first in spirit.

Large illustrated compositions may:

- recompose;
- crop intentionally;
- rearrange;
- simplify;
- hide secondary decorative objects.

Do not reduce complex poster art until every element becomes microscopic.

Kitchen HQ may use substantially different responsive behavior because its information density is higher.

---

# 58. Mobile Shmifter Experience

On mobile:

- one primary action should dominate;
- text must remain comfortable;
- voting must feel tactile;
- shift selection must remain obvious;
- decorative art should surround rather than suffocate content.

The experience should still feel visually rich.

Do not solve mobile by stripping away all personality.

---

# 59. Desktop Shmifter Experience

Desktop allows larger world-building compositions.

Use space to create:

- scenes;
- layered objects;
- illustrated framing;
- richer relationships between content and art.

Avoid simply centering a narrow mobile column in an enormous empty charcoal screen.

---

# 60. Kitchen HQ Responsive Behavior

Kitchen HQ should be optimized primarily for actual kitchen planning work.

Large screens may expose:

- wider tables;
- side-by-side information;
- filters;
- summaries;
- operational panels.

Small screens should prioritize essential review and lightweight editing.

Do not force desktop-level data density into an unusable mobile layout.

---

# 61. Accessibility

The visual world is expressive.

The product must still be usable.

Requirements include:

- sufficient text contrast;
- readable type sizes;
- visible focus states;
- keyboard accessibility;
- meaningful labels;
- non-color status communication;
- reduced motion support;
- touch-friendly targets.

Do not use "art direction" as justification for poor accessibility.

---

# 62. Visual Priority

When design goals conflict, use this order:

### Safety

Allergies and critical information.

### Usability

Can the user complete the task?

### Clarity

Can they understand the information?

### Shmifting Identity

Does it belong to the world?

### Delight

Does it create joy?

A delightful interaction that compromises safety or usability is wrong.

# 64. Anti-Patterns

The following outcomes should trigger redesign:

### "Dark SaaS"

A standard dashboard with charcoal background.

### "Festival Template"

Generic neon, gradients, tribal patterns and Burning Man clichés.

### "Children's Game"

Cute mascots and excessive reward behavior.

### "Corporate Psychedelia"

Perfect geometric layouts with colorful blobs.

### "Illustrated Spreadsheet"

Dense admin content covered with decorative characters.

### "Art Project"

Beautiful screen that is frustrating to operate.

### "Random Weird"

Surreal elements without a coherent relationship to Shmifting.

**Kitchen HQ**

Defines:

# 66. Visual Review Rule

A screen is not approved merely because:

- it works;
- it is responsive;
- it uses the correct colors;
- it includes Shmifting assets.

Ask:

> **If the SHMIFTING logo disappeared, would this still unmistakably belong to the same universe as the Golden References?**

If not, the design is not finished.

---

# 67. Design Review Questions

For every major screen, review:

### World

Does this feel like Shmifting?

### Hierarchy

Is the most important thing obvious?

### Humanity

Does this feel made for people rather than data?

### Weirdness

Is there enough unexpected character?

### Restraint

Is the weirdness controlled?

### Function

Can the task be completed easily?

### Consistency

Does it match the Golden References?

### Density

Is the balance appropriate for member vs Admin experience?

# 68. The Home Test

The Shmifter Home is the first major visual benchmark.

It should immediately communicate:

**SHMIFTING**

**Countdown to the desert**

**Human warmth**

**Kitchen preparation**

**What I need to do next**

without feeling like a dashboard.

If Home fails this test, do not continue designing the rest of the member experience.

Fix the language first.

---

# 69. The Voting Test

Voting should answer:

> Could this interaction exist only in Shmifting?

If it feels interchangeable with a generic polling app, it needs more design work.

If it becomes so artistic that voting is unclear, it also needs more design work.

The correct point is:

**distinctive + obvious.**

---

# 70. The Kitchen HQ Test

Kitchen HQ should pass a different test:

> Could the Kitchen Lead work here for an hour without fighting the design?

If not, simplify.

Then ask:

> Does it still clearly belong to Shmifting?

If not, restore identity through the environment rather than by compromising data usability.

# 71. The Final Moment

The visual journey should culminate in:

# LOCK THE KITCHEN

This moment can be more theatrical than normal Admin actions.

It represents the transition from:

**planning**

to

**living.**

After the kitchen is locked, the product should visually communicate completion and calm.

Not:

> Keep engaging with the app!

But:

> We are ready.

---

# 72. The Design Paradox

The entire Shmifting product is built around a useful paradox:

We are using technology to organize an experience whose ultimate goal includes **leaving technology behind**.

The visual design should respect this.

Shmifting should feel:

- handmade;
- physical;
- tactile;
- imperfect;
- human.

Not:

- futuristic;
- automated;
- AI-centric;
- technologically impressive for its own sake.

The technology should disappear behind the experience.

---

# 73. Relationship to the Product Bible

When designing, always consult:

`SHMIFTING_PRODUCT_BIBLE.md`

The Design Book must not override product requirements.

Likewise, the Product Bible must not be used as an excuse to ignore the established visual world.

Together:

**Product Bible = WHAT + WHY**

**Design Book = HOW IT FEELS**

**Golden References = WHAT "RIGHT" LOOKS LIKE**

# 74. Instructions to Claude Code

Before implementing a significant UI:

1. Read the relevant Product Bible requirements.
2. Read this Design Book.
3. Inspect all current Golden References.
4. Identify whether the screen is primarily **World** or **Interface**.
5. Determine its correct visual density.
6. Reuse established Shmifting patterns and assets.
7. Implement functionality.
8. Compare the result visually against the Golden References.
9. Refine before moving on.

Do not rush from functional completion to the next screen.

Visual fidelity is part of completion.

---

# 75. Creative Freedom

Claude is allowed and expected to solve implementation-level design problems.

Claude may:

- improve layout;
- solve responsive behavior;
- create sensible component structures;
- refine hierarchy;
- choose appropriate interaction patterns;
- simplify where necessary for usability.

Claude should not:

- redefine the brand;
- replace the illustration language;
- introduce another aesthetic;
- turn the member experience into conventional SaaS;
- ignore the Golden References because another solution is easier to code.

When uncertain:

> **Extend the established language. Do not replace it.**

---

# 76. Definition of Done — Visual

A screen is visually complete when:

- it fulfills its product task;
- it is usable;
- Hebrew behaves correctly;
- it works at relevant screen sizes;
- accessibility is respected;
- it uses the Shmifting visual grammar;
- it feels consistent with Golden References;
- illustration and interface feel integrated;
- motion, when present, has purpose;
- it avoids generic SaaS patterns;
- it remains recognizably Shmifting without depending on the logo.

When making a difficult visual decision, remember what we are actually creating.

Not a dashboard.

Not a food-management SaaS.

Not a Midburn website.

We are building a strange, warm, illustrated little world created by a group of people preparing to feed one another in the desert.

The interface happens to live inside it.

# PEOPLE FIRST.

# FEED THEM WELL.

# MAKE IT WEIRD.

# KEEP IT HUMAN.

**Feeding people is our gift.**
